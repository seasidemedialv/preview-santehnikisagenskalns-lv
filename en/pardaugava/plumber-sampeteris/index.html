Plumber in Šampēteris, Riga - Soviet-era blocks, terraced houses and wooden buildings

A plumber in Šampēteris, Riga, meets three different kinds of building within one neighbourhood: Soviet-era series blocks of flats, terraced houses from the 1990s and wooden houses from the early 20th century.

The residential streets are in the south-west of the neighbourhood between Šampētera, Zasulauka and Kalnciema iela, while most of its 136.6 ha is taken up by mixed, mostly industrial development. Each type of building has its own main plumbing question: the riser, the boundary of the work or the pipe by the outer wall.

Series blocks by Irlavas, Stendes and Zalves iela

The Soviet-era series blocks stand at the junction of Irlavas, Stendes and Zalves iela, next to houses with a better layout built in the 1980s. In such a building shared cold and hot water risers and a soil stack run through all floors, and in the bathroom the heated towel rail is often connected directly to the hot water riser.

If the building still has its original risers, a bathroom renovation usually starts with the question of whether to replace your own floor section as well. The riser is common property, so Section 11 of the Apartment Ownership Law requires the consent of the flat owners' association. Section 10 obliges you to let into the flat the specialists authorised by the building manager if the work is needed for the whole building. In practice: first talk to the building manager, then plan the tiles and the box around the riser so that it stays accessible.

In a small bathroom the bath is often replaced with a shower. The price of the plumbing part depends on carrying out the old bath, on whether it will be a cabin, a tray or a shower with a drain, and on whether the sewer outlet has to be lowered or moved for the fall. Tiling and waterproofing are another trade.

Heating in a series block: a radiator is not only the flat's business

According to an e-consultation on the LV portāls website, replacing radiators in a block of flats must be agreed with the building manager, and without a calculation you may not significantly increase the radiator's output or change the connection scheme. Section 4 of the Apartment Ownership Law counts heating elements whose operation depends on the building's system as common property.

In the heating season the heating riser is full, so a radiator is replaced when the building manager shuts it off and drains it. In buildings run by Rīgas namu pārvaldnieks, heating starts when, for at least three days in a row, the air has not warmed above +14 degrees and has at some point cooled to +8. It is more convenient to plan a radiator replacement before that. A heated towel rail on the hot water riser does not depend on the heating season: that riser is full all year, and shutting it off at any time is agreed with the building manager.

Terraced houses at the corner of Zalves and Irlavas iela

A terraced-house project was built in the 1990s at the corner of Zalves and Irlavas iela. In a terraced house the work starts with the question of what belongs to each section and what is shared: the incoming water supply, the meter assembly, the sewer outlet to the yard chamber. That is determined by the particular building's design and documents, not by how the building looks.

If the outlet is shared, a blockage in one section can show up in a neighbour's floor drain or toilet. Then the cause has to be sought at the stretch where the sections' pipes join, and the work has to be discussed with the neighbours. Riga's regulations connect the whole property to the city system, not individual flats, so there is usually one connection for the whole building.

Swiss-style wooden houses on Volguntes and Candera iela

The oldest street network consists of Volguntes, Zalves, Stendes, Šampētera and Jūrkalnes iela, parallel to Kalnciema iela. In the early 20th century, wooden residential houses in the Swiss style were built here, for example on Frīdriha Candera iela and Volguntes iela, and modest workers' houses have also survived.

  • Pipes along an outer wall or in an unheated pantry freeze first in a hard frost, so when replacing a pipe it is better to route it along an inner wall.
  • A wooden partition often does not carry the weight of a water heater without extra fixings, so the heater is hung on a load-bearing wall or a frame.
  • An old house often has flats with plumbing from different periods, so steel, copper and plastic pipes can meet in one building, and the joints have to be checked.
  • A sewer outlet under a wooden floor: the floor joists determine where a new pipe can be run and where it has enough fall.

Read more about such buildings in the guide plumbing in an old wooden house.

Groundwater by the Jūrmala railway line

Groundwater in Šampēteris mostly lies 1.5–3 m deep. Closer to the Jūrmala railway line, in the middle and east of the neighbourhood, groundwater is shallower than 1.5 m. The highest places, up to 17 m above sea level, are the Zasulauks dunes between Šampētera, Zasulauka and Kalnciema iela. For a house with a basement closer to the railway, it is good to know that basement appliances below the level of the inspection chamber lid need a non-return valve, and groundwater may not be discharged into the drains without approval.

What people usually ask

We are renovating a bathroom in a building on Irlavas iela. May I replace the riser only on my own floor?

Technically a one-floor section can be replaced, but the riser is common property, so the consent of the flat owners' association is needed, which the building manager usually arranges. The manager also shuts off the riser. If the neighbours plan to replace the whole riser, it is better to wait or join in.

In a wooden house the kitchen pipe freezes in winter. What should I do?

Shut off the water at the incoming supply pipe and thaw the pipe slowly with warm air, not an open flame. If the pipe has burst, water will appear as soon as it thaws, so the valve must stay closed. In the long term it helps to move the pipe away from the outer wall.

Can a heated towel rail be replaced during the heating season?

If the rail is connected to the hot water riser, the heating season does not affect the work, but the riser has to be shut off, and the time is agreed with the building manager. If it is connected to the heating riser, the work is the same as with a radiator. In the form, state what the rail is connected to, if you know.
