Plumber in Klīversala, Riga - perimeter blocks on soft ground

A plumber in Klīversala, Riga, works mainly in blocks of flats: Klīversala (part of the Āgenskalns neighbourhood) is a former island between Āgenskalns Bay and the Kīleveins ditch, and its development is perimeter-style, as is typical of central Riga.

The buildings stand tight against the street, the flats share risers, and the basements sit on ground where groundwater is close to the surface. Three questions follow from that: who is responsible for the riser, where the blockage is and what may be installed in a basement.

When the neighbour's water appears in your bath

In a perimeter-style building the drains from all floors come together in one soil stack and, in the basement, continue in one pipe to the yard or the street. If only your sink does not drain, the blockage is usually in the trap or in the short pipe to the riser, and it can be cleared in the flat. If water rises in the bath or shower when someone upstairs uses water, the blockage is most likely in the soil stack or in the basement pipe below you.

Under the Apartment Ownership Law, your part extends up to the riser, but the riser and the basement pipes are the building's services. Under the Residential Property Management Law, maintaining them is the building manager's task. So in the second case, first report it to the building manager and until then do not pour water down, so that you do not flood your own bathroom.

Clearing a soil stack often needs access to the cleanout hatch, which is located in one of the flats. Under the law, the owner must let in the specialists sent by the building manager if the work is needed to maintain the common services, so do not wall the hatch in with tiles and leave free space by it.

Apartment Ownership Law, Sections 3 and 10; Residential Property Management Law, Section 6. Checked 16.11.2026.

Silt tens of metres deep and groundwater closer than one and a half metres

In the eastern part of Āgenskalns, where Uzvaras parks and Klīversala are, layers of sand 1.5–6 m thick alternate with 3–10 m thick silt, in places to a depth of 20–30 m, and groundwater is shallower than 1.5 m. The former bank of the Daugava is marked here by the Raņķa dambis embankment, built in the middle of the 19th century.

For a basement room that means the plumbing there is close to the groundwater level and may be lower than the lid of the yard chamber. Riga's regulations forbid connecting sinks, toilets, floor drains or drainage that are lower than the lid of the nearest inspection chamber to the drains without a non-return valve or an electrically operated gate valve. Before you set up a laundry room or a toilet in the basement, this height has to be measured.

If water collects in the basement, it may not simply be pumped into the household drains: discharging groundwater and rainwater needs approval. For the building's shared systems in the basement, talk to the building manager.

Riga binding regulations No. 17: a non-return valve for low appliances (point 63.8), groundwater only with approval (point 63.9). Checked 16.11.2026.

A radiator in a flat is part of the building's system

If the building is connected to district heating, the radiators in the flat run from the shared heating riser and by law form part of the common property. Replacing a radiator must be agreed with the building manager, and without a calculation you may not significantly increase the radiator's output or change the connection scheme.

In buildings run by Rīgas namu pārvaldnieks, heating starts according to the weather: when for at least three days and nights the air has not warmed above +14 degrees and at some point has cooled to +8, and similar weather is expected to continue. Flat owners can agree to connect the heating earlier or later. So it is more convenient to plan a radiator replacement outside the heating season; shutting off and draining the riser is in any case agreed with the building manager.

Apartment Ownership Law, Section 4; the LV portāls e-consultation; LSM on the heating season. Checked 16.11.2026.

If the building is being built or rebuilt

The north-western part of Klīversala by Āgenskalns Bay has been given the status of a centre development area, which allows large-scale building. In a new or completely rebuilt flat there is no old-riser issue; plumbers are usually called there to install appliances: a wall-hung toilet with a frame, a sink, a shower or a water heater, and after installation the pipes are pressure-tested.

If a new building that uses water is built on a plot up to whose boundary a Rīgas ūdens service pipe has been laid, connecting to the central system is mandatory, and the whole property is connected, not individual flats.

New-build connection and the whole property as one connection: binding regulations No. 17, points 5 and 7, likumi.lv 16.11.2026.

What people usually ask

Is Klīversala a separate neighbourhood to write in the request?

No, Klīversala is a former island and a part of the city within the Āgenskalns neighbourhood. In the request write the exact address with the street and house number, and then it does not matter which of the two names you choose.

May I set up a shower in the basement if it is lower than the yard chamber?

Only with a non-return valve or an electrically operated gate valve, as Riga's regulations No. 17 require. The basement pipes are the building's common services, so first talk to the building manager.

Who calls the plumber if a basement pipe is blocked?

Maintenance of the building's shared pipes is the building manager's task, so usually the manager organises the plumber. If the blockage is in your pipe up to the riser, you can call a plumber yourself; clearing a blockage in the internal drains is from €50.
