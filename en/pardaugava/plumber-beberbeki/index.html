The water supply and drains in the streets were built in phase 5 of the Cohesion Fund project, so for many houses switching to the city network is the live question. The other side is the variety of houses: large two-storey private houses and garden cabins that people live in through the winter too.

The fifth-phase network in the street: what has to be switched over in the house

In the project “Water Management Development in Riga”, the water supply and drains in Beberbeķi were built in phase 5 together with Berģi and Imanta. In 2025 residents of Beberbeķi were among those who connected to the centralised networks most actively. The Rīgas ūdens network ends at the outer wall of the meter chamber or inspection chamber, or at the plot boundary; beyond that the pipes are the owner's responsibility.

  1. Incoming supply pipe and the meter assembly The new water pipe enters the house, and after the meter it is joined to the existing internal pipe. The place has to be chosen where the pipe does not freeze.
  2. Separating the old well or borehole Riga's regulations forbid connecting a borehole system to the city water supply; follow the same principle for a well too. If you keep the pressure booster pump and the well for the garden, their pipes must be completely separate.
  3. The drain outlet from the house The pipes that ran to the septic tank or holding tank are switched to the new line up to the inspection chamber. A drain from the cellar needs a fall of at least 2 cm per metre.
  4. Internal points If a bathroom or boiler room is being rebuilt at the same time, new water and drain outlets are built now, while the walls and floor are already open.

Beberbeķi, as a phase 5 and 6 area of the Cohesion Fund project, has separate binding regulations: co-financing of up to €739 for design and up to €1,800 or the whole cost of building the connection, with specific deadlines. The general Riga regulations RD-23-207-sn do not apply to these networks, and no co-financing is provided for a water supply connection. Check the deadlines with Rīgas ūdens. Checked 16.11.2026.

A two-storey house with a garden and a cabin lived in through winter

The buildings in Beberbeķi are very mixed: beside allotments and modest huts that people live in through the winter too stand large, mostly two-storey private houses with fenced gardens. The plumbing needs of these buildings are completely different.

In a two-storey house with two bathrooms and a boiler room, the key is that the water heater and the heating loop match the consumption, and that the second-floor drains reach the outlet without long horizontal sections. In a small cabin with one sink and a shower the main thing is frost: pipes under the floor and along the outer wall can freeze if the heating stops in the cold.

If you leave the house unheated for a while, manufacturers (for example Dražice) advise draining the water heater before the cold, disconnecting it from the electricity and also draining the water pipes up to a point where they do not freeze. For a pressure booster pump, if there is one, the manufacturer Grundfos advises draining it together with the tank when there is a risk of frost.

Shallow groundwater, but no flood risk

Building conditions in Beberbeķi are generally favourable and there is practically no flood risk, but the groundwater is shallower than 1.5 m. That means the floor of a cellar or utility room can be close to the water, and in spring damp can appear there.

  • For a cellar pump with a float switch, if it is installed permanently, the manufacturer Grundfos requires a non-return valve in the discharge pipe.
  • Groundwater and rainwater may not be discharged into the city drains without approval (regulations No. 17, clause 63.9).
  • Fixtures in a cellar that are lower than the cover of the nearest inspection chamber need a separate outlet and a valve against backflow.
  • Where a pipe passes through the foundations in wet ground, the building code requires a seal.

The Beberbeķi lines and the way over Ulmaņa gatve

Nine streets in Beberbeķi were given the names of Beberbeķi lines in 1926; the others are Čuguna, Nīgrandes, Virgas, Gaviezes, Krotes and Turlavas iela. In the form, enter the line number or the street and the house number: the line names are similar, and the exact address helps find the house. Kārļa Ulmaņa gatve crosses the northern part of the neighbourhood, and there has been a pedestrian bridge over it since 2009.

What people usually ask

After connecting, can I keep the old well for watering the garden?

Riga's binding regulations No. 17 forbid connecting a borehole system to the city water supply, and it is worth following the same principle for a well. If you keep the well, its pump and pipes must be completely separate from the house's new water supply, with no shared tap or switchable valve.

What co-financing is there for a connection in Beberbeķi?

Beberbeķi has separate phase 5 and 6 regulations: up to €739 for design and up to €1,800 or the whole cost of the build, with deadlines. Ask Rīgas ūdens for the exact conditions and deadlines, as they are time-limited.

We will not live in the garden house over winter. How do I prepare the plumbing?

Turn off the water at the incoming supply pipe, drain the pipes up to a point where they do not freeze, drain the water heater and disconnect it from the electricity. If there is a pressure booster pump, drain that too. In spring, look over the joints before turning the water on.
