Plumber in Bieriņi, Riga - private houses, the Mārupīte and new service pipes

A plumber in Bieriņi mostly works in private houses: more than half of the neighbourhood is detached houses with gardens, and in recent years many houses have been switching from a septic tank or holding tank to the city drains.

So the work here usually takes place inside the house, in the cellar and in the yard, not at a shared riser. Bieriņi also includes Ozolciems, Petriņciems and the part of the city called Mārupe (within Riga).

Mārupe within Riga's boundaries is not Mārupe municipality

The Bieriņi neighbourhood includes three parts of the city: Mārupe (within Riga), Ozolciems and Petriņciems. When Rīgas ūdens notices write about networks in “Mārupe (within Riga)”, they mean the part of Bieriņi, not Mārupe municipality beyond the city boundary. Riga City Council binding regulations on water services apply to these houses, and in phase 4 Rīgas ūdens built new networks there; whether they are in your street can be checked on vaitevtovajag.lv.

In the form, enter the street and house number, not just the word “Mārupe”. From the address the specialist understands that the house is in Bieriņi, and on the call can immediately ask about what matters for this place: whether the service pipe already reaches the fence, whether the house has a cellar and where the inspection chamber is.

The branch at the fence: where Rīgas ūdens ends and the house side begins

In phase 4 of the Cohesion Fund project “Water Management Development in Riga”, in 2012–2015 Rīgas ūdens built new networks in the Mārupe (within Riga) part too. Together with Katlakalns and Bolderāja, sewerage became available to 6,000 residents. In 2025 residents of Bieriņi were among those who connected to the centralised networks most actively.

Under Riga's binding regulations No. 17, the company's sewer network ends at the outer wall of the inspection chamber. Everything beyond it belongs to the owner and is their responsibility: the pipe from the chamber to the house, the place where it passes through the foundations and all the internal drains. Building the service pipe to the chamber is a project with approvals, arranged together with Rīgas ūdens. The plumber usually takes the inside of the house and its connection to the new line.

  • Passing through the foundations or the cellar wall: the building code requires a gap around the pipe with a flexible, watertight filling, and in wet ground a seal.
  • The outlet from the cellar: a slope of at least 20 ‰, that is 2 cm per metre, and a diameter not smaller than the largest connected riser.
  • The old internal pipes that so far ran to the septic tank: they are joined to the new outlet so that the whole house drains into one line.
  • New points in a bathroom or kitchen, if the rebuild is done at the same time as the connection.

Riga City Council binding regulations No. RD-23-207-sn: co-financing for connecting a residential house to the centralised sewerage is €1,000 for a service pipe up to 5 m, €1,500 for 5–20 m and €2,000 over 20 m. It is paid out after construction, if at least one person is declared as resident at the property and a contract has been signed with Rīgas ūdens. The whole process from technical conditions to handover usually takes about 4–6 months. Checked 16.11.2026.

Peat in the Mārupīte valley and a cellar below the street chamber cover

The Mārupīte flows from the Mārupe side towards Āgenskalns. In its valley and in the valley of Bieriņgrāvis, also called Melnupīte, there are peat deposits 1–2 m, in places 3 m and thicker, and the lowest point of the neighbourhood, 7 m above sea level, is right in the Mārupīte valley. In the north-western part the groundwater is shallower than 1.5 m.

For a house with a cellar this changes two things. If the cellar has a shower, washing machine or toilet and it is lower than the cover of the nearest inspection chamber, the building code requires a separate outlet and a device that stops the external sewer from flooding the cellar. Riga's regulations forbid connecting such fixtures without a non-return valve or an electrically operated gate valve. Second, groundwater from a cellar pump or drainage may not be discharged into the drains without approval, so do not take the pump hose to the nearest sink.

In peat, pipes in the ground can settle unevenly, so the bedding and the joints matter for a yard drain. If a pipe starts to block in the same place after a few years, it is cleaned first, but the cause of repeated problems can be found with a camera inspection before digging.

A boiler, a water heater and radiators that belong only to you

A private house has no building manager and no shared riser in the basement. That simplifies the work: you do not have to wait for the neighbours' consent, and the heating loop can be drained when it suits you. In return, the pressure in the system, the air in the radiators and the condition of the water heater are looked after by the owner.

If the water is heated by a heating boiler, the water heater contains a heat exchanger, that is a pipe coil, and its connections are part of the heating loop. An electric wall-hung water heater collects limescale over time, and cleaning means draining, opening and checking the seal. In a boiler room several things can be looked at in one call, so list everything that bothers you in the form.

What people usually ask

The house is in the part of the city called Mārupe within Riga's boundaries. Is that Bieriņi?

Yes. The part of the city Mārupe (within Riga) is part of the Bieriņi neighbourhood, as are Ozolciems and Petriņciems. If the address says “Mārupes pag.” or “Mārupes nov.”, the house is in Mārupe municipality, outside Riga, which is a different municipality with its own rules. For a Riga address, choose Bieriņi in the form and enter the full address.

The service pipe to the chamber by the fence is already there. What does the plumber do in the house?

Usually the house side: the outlet through the foundations or the cellar wall, joining the internal pipes into one line, protecting cellar fixtures against backflow and new points if they are needed. Exactly what has to be done depends on how the house's drains were built until now.

May a cellar drainage pump be connected to the drains?

Riga's binding regulations No. 17 forbid discharging groundwater or rainwater into the drains without approval. If groundwater collects in the cellar, its discharge has to be solved another way or approved with Rīgas ūdens.

What should I enter in the form if the problem is in the cellar?

What is happening (leaking, smelling, water standing in the drain), whether the cellar has a shower, toilet or washing machine, and whether the house is already connected to the city drains. If you know where the inspection chamber is, note that too.
