Flooded flat - when the water has reached the neighbour below

A flooded flat in a Pārdaugava block of flats, Riga's left bank, almost every time means one of three sources: your pipe or appliance, the shared riser, or the neighbour a floor above.

First close the shut-off valves in your flat and go down to the neighbour below to see whether the leak stops. The plumber finds the leak and fixes it, but the building's riser is touched only with the building manager's knowledge.

How to tell which floor the water comes from

  • When you close your valves, the dripping at the neighbour below stops after a while: the source is in your flat, beyond the valves.
  • It still leaks with the valves closed, and your own ceiling is getting damp too: the water comes from above.
  • The stains are only around the riser box, on several floors in a row: the fault is the riser or its joint with the branch pipe.
  • The neighbour gets a leak only when you wash or the washing machine runs: the drains are leaking, not the water supply. The valves do not stop it, because drain pipes are not under pressure.
  • Damp under the tiles along the edge of the bath: the seal between the bath and the wall, or the bath's waste outlet.

In a wooden intermediate floor, as in the old houses of Āgenskalns and Zasulauks, water can run along the beams and show up a long way from where it escapes. So a stain in the neighbour's room does not yet mean the pipe is directly above it.

How the plumber finds and fixes the leak

  1. Pressure pipes All taps are closed and the water meters are watched. If the reading moves, water is escaping in the pipes beyond the meter, and the search continues along them.
  2. Drains, one appliance at a time Water is run in turn in the sink, the bath, the shower and the toilet, checking each time whether water appears at the neighbour below. This finds faults in the waste pipes and traps, not in the supply pipes.
  3. Access The bath panel is removed, and the hatch in the riser box or the sink cupboard is opened. If the pipe is behind tiles with no hatch, opening it is agreed before the work.
  4. Repair A seal, a joint, a trap or a short section of pipe is replaced. If the riser is leaking, the work is agreed with the building manager, who shuts off the riser.
  5. Test under load The valves are opened, the appliances are run again, and the joints and the ceiling below are checked to stay dry.

The neighbour below, the building manager and the flooding report

The Apartment Ownership Law counts the pipes up to the common-property risers and the bathroom fittings as part of the flat, while the risers themselves and the building's systems are common property. So a leak in your branch pipe beyond the riser is usually a matter for your flat, while a leak in the riser is a matter for the building, unless the building's documents say otherwise. Ask the building manager who covers the damage in your particular case.

The flooding report (a written record of the water damage) notes when and from where the water came and what it damaged. It helps when the neighbours or the building later discuss the damage. Photograph the ceilings, walls and the leak spot before they dry or are repaired. How to prepare the report and who signs it is described in the guide water leaking from the flat above.

If the water has run along the ceiling near light fittings or sockets, the neighbour below should not switch those on until an electrician has checked that it is not dangerous.

Apartment Ownership Law, Section 3: the elements of a flat are the utility lines up to the common-property risers and the bath, shower and toilet fittings; Section 4: the building's utility systems are common property. Checked 16.11.2026.

What people usually ask

The neighbour below says it leaks from us, but everything is dry here. How can that be?

Water can run under the bath or in the floor where you cannot see it, for example from the bath's waste outlet or a drain joint that leaks only when water is used. It can equally come from the floor above you and run down along the riser through your floor. This is found by running the appliances one at a time and watching when water appears below.

The neighbour above flooded us, but he cannot be reached. What should I do?

Contact the building manager, because he can shut off the riser and stop the water for the whole vertical line. The Apartment Ownership Law obliges an owner to let in specialists authorised by the manager if work is needed for the building's utilities to operate; how to deal with a locked flat is decided by the building manager.

Can a riser leak be fixed on my order?

The riser is common property, and work that affects it usually needs the consent of the flat owners' association or the building manager. Ask the building manager: if he agrees and shuts off the riser, the plumber can replace the damaged section on your floor.

After flooding, do I also need to check my own pipes?

If the water came from above, your pipes are usually not affected. It is worth checking if water stood on the floor by the washing machine or water heater connection, and any electrical appliances that got wet should be checked by an electrician before use.
