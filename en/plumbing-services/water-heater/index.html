In the bathroom of a Soviet-era flat the water heater often hangs above the toilet or the washing machine, in a wooden house on a partition of plaster on laths that was not built for such a load. The plumber installs or replaces a wall-hung water heater, connects it with a safety valve, removes limescale and replaces a faulty heating element, thermostat or anode.

Where a water heater can hang in a flat and in an old wooden house

A full water heater weighs much more than an empty one, because the tank holds all the water. So what matters most is what it is fixed to. In a brick or concrete wall anchors hold well. In Soviet-era series blocks, some of the partitions in bathrooms and toilets are thin, and a water heater is not hung on plasterboard lining: the fixing must reach the load-bearing wall, or a frame is used.

In an old wooden house in Āgenskalns or Ķīpsala, a partition is often plaster on laths over boards or logs. The water heater is fixed to a log or a standing stud, not to the lining. If there is none, a support is added to the wall, or another place is chosen, for example the inside of an outer wall or a cupboard in the hall. More about such houses is in the guide plumbing in an old wooden house.

In a block of flats you also have to look at what is under the water heater. If the tank or its joint starts to leak, the water runs along the floor to the neighbour below. The supply pipes must be accessible, so that they can be shut off without breaking open a box.

Central hot water in the building and a water heater as a reserve

In many Pārdaugava blocks of flats, hot water comes from the building's heat substation, and a water heater is not obligatory there. Still, people keep one as a reserve, because hot water tends to be switched off during repairs or when the substation is serviced. In such a flat the water heater is connected to the cold water and to the hot water network with valves, so that you can switch over.

If a water heater stands unused for months, the water in it stands too. According to SPKC (the Centre for Disease Prevention and Control), warm water from +20 °C to +45 °C, including in a water heater tank, is an environment in which legionella multiplies quickly, so hot water at every tap must be warmer than +50 °C. Before a reserve water heater is put into use, the water is heated to full temperature and the taps are flushed.

Water heater cleaning: hissing, slow heating, smelly hot water

Limescale builds up on the tank walls and the heating element. How fast depends on the hardness of the water, the temperature set and how much hot water is used; the manufacturer Dražice advises a lower temperature so there is less deposit. When the layer of scale is thick, the water heater heats more slowly, hissing can be heard in the tank, and white grains can appear at the tap.

Cleaning a water heater means draining the tank, opening the flange, removing the scale and checking the gasket. Dražice advises having a specialist remove the limescale after 1–2 years of operation if the water has many minerals, and not to clean an enamelled tank with acids. At the same time the magnesium anode is inspected: Dražice and Ariston state that it should be checked or replaced after 2 years. On health grounds, SPKC advises cleaning and disinfecting the water heater at least once a year.

Dražice OKCE/OKHE and Ariston Velis Evo installation manuals; SPKC recommendations for limiting the spread of Legionella in flats. Checked 13.11.2026.

The safety valve: when dripping is normal and when it is not

Every pressurised water heater must have a safety valve on the cold water supply, and the manufacturer Dražice states that no shut-off tap may be fitted between the water heater and the valve. Water expands as it heats, so the valve usually drips during heating, and that is normal. It is another matter if it drips also when the heating element is off and the taps are closed: then the suspicion falls on too much pressure in the pipes or on a worn valve.

The valve's drain pipe must be open to the air and pointing down, usually to a trap or the drains. It must not be plugged, even if the dripping is annoying. If it drips constantly, see water heater repair.

Before the plumber comes: the wall, the taps and the socket

  • Decide the volume and the place, and if you can, find out the wall material: brick, concrete, plasterboard or wood.
  • Check whether the flat's water valves at the riser close; if not, only the building manager can do this.
  • Disconnect the old water heater from the electricity and let the water cool.
  • Clear the space under the water heater and the way to carry out the old tank.
  • If the water heater needs a new socket or line, arrange an electrician.

What people usually ask

May a water heater be hung on a plasterboard partition?

Not on the plasterboard itself. The fixing must reach the load-bearing wall, or the water heater is hung on a frame. If that is not possible, it is better to choose another place or a smaller tank.

The building has central hot water. Is a water heater needed at all?

It is not obligatory, but many people keep one as a reserve for when the hot water is switched off. Before using a reserve water heater after a long pause, the water is heated to full temperature and the taps are flushed.

In a wooden house the wall is plaster on laths. Where should the water heater be fixed?

To a log or a standing stud, not to the plaster. If there is none in the right place, a support is added to the wall, or the water heater goes elsewhere, for example in a cupboard in the hall.

May the water heater's safety valve drip?

During heating, yes, because the water expands. If the water heater has stopped heating and nobody is drawing water, but the valve drips, the pressure is usually too high or the valve is faulty.
