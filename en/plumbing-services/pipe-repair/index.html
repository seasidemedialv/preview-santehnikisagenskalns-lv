In a flat the plumber works on your branch beyond the valves, and touches the riser only after agreeing with the building manager. In a wooden house with its own incoming supply, the whole stretch from the meter pit to the last tap is usually the owner's responsibility.

Where your pipes end: the riser, the valve, the meter pit

The Apartment Ownership Law counts the utility lines up to the common-property risers as part of the flat. In practice this means: the pipes from the riser branch through the valves and meters to the sink, the bath and the washing machine are yours, and can be replaced without a decision of the flat owners' association. The riser and its connection are part of the building, so work that touches it is agreed with the building manager.

In Pārdaugava, Riga's left bank, pre-war brick buildings, Soviet-era series blocks and new developments stand side by side in one street. Āgenskalna priedes, the first large Soviet residential estate in Riga, was built in 1958–1962, and flats in such buildings often still have the original steel pipes. Once the zinc wears away on galvanised steel, rust builds up inside, the pipe narrows, the pressure falls and the water turns brownish.

In wooden houses in Āgenskalns, Torņakalns and Zasulauks, pipes tend to run along outer walls and through an unheated basement. The building code requires rooms with cold water pipes to be kept above 2 °C or the pipes to be protected from frost. The responsibility of Rīgas ūdens usually ends at the outer wall of the meter pit or at the plot boundary; the stretch in the yard up to the house and everything in the house is the owner's responsibility.

Apartment Ownership Law, Sections 3 and 11. Riga City Council binding regulations No. 17, clause 3: the Rīgas ūdens network reaches the outer wall of the commercial metering inspection chamber (1.5–2 m from the street's red line) or the plot boundary. LBN 221-15, clause 109: rooms with cold water pipes above 2 °C. Checked 16.11.2026.

Installing pipes: which pipe to fit in place of old steel

In flats, plastic pipes are usually fitted in place of old steel: multilayer (with an aluminium layer), PEX or polypropylene. They differ in how much they expand in hot water. For ordinary polypropylene pipe the manufacturer aquatherm states 0.15 mm per metre per degree and advises compensation after as little as 10 m of straight run, while Uponor states 0.025 mm for its multilayer pipe, about six times less than PEX.

In a small Soviet-era bathroom a hot water pipe along the wall therefore needs room to expand. The building code requires a flat's pipes and fittings to be chosen for a working pressure of at least 600 kPa.

Expansion: aquatherm blue installation principles; Uponor MLC product information. LBN 221-15, clause 117 (600 kPa). Checked 13.11.2026.

Replacing valves: when the old valve no longer shuts off the water

A valve that has not been closed for years is often stuck: the handle does not turn, or water leaks past it even when closed. It is worth testing before an emergency, by closing the valve and opening it again.

A valve after the meter or at the washing machine is replaced by closing the flat's main valve. The first valve directly after the riser is different: to unscrew it the whole riser has to be shut off, and then the neighbours are without water too. The time is agreed with the building manager.

Pipes rumble or hum when a tap is turned

Noise in pipes is usually a sign that somewhere the water has too narrow a passage or too much pressure. The most common causes in a flat:

  • a valve is open only partly, and the water squeezes through it with a whistle;
  • a worn gasket in a tap or valve starts to vibrate;
  • after the riser was shut off, air is left in the pipes and comes out with knocking;
  • a pipe is not fixed and bangs against the wall when the washing machine closes its valve sharply;
  • the pressure in the building is high.

Under LBN 221-15, the pressure at the lowest water draw-off point must not exceed 6 bar. Where the pressure is higher, a pressure reducer after the incoming valve helps: it keeps the outlet pressure steady and reduces flow noise. In a block of flats, high pressure is the building manager's matter; in a private or wooden house the owner fits the reducer.

LBN 221-15, clause 35: pressure at the lowest point up to 0.6 MPa (6 bar). Reducer operation per EN 806-2: Resideo Braukmann D04FM specification. Checked 13.11.2026.

Pressure testing pipes before the box is closed

For a new water supply system the building code requires a pressure test, flushing with drinking water and, if needed, disinfection. The test pressure is stated in the design, or taken from the standard LVS EN 806-4. The standard advises a pressure 1.1 times the maximum design pressure, for example 8.25 bar if the working pressure is 5 bar.

Manufacturers state their own procedure. For its pipes Uponor states a water test in two stages: first 11 bar held for 30 minutes without the pressure falling, then 5.5 bar for 120 minutes. In a flat it is worth doing while the pipes are still visible, before they are boxed in with plasterboard or tiled over.

LBN 221-15, clause 13.1; WRAS explanation of EN 806 (1.1 x MDP); Uponor pressure test report 1120604. Checked 13.11.2026.

Before the plumber comes: valves, the box and the neighbours

  • Check that the flat's valves close, and note down any that do not turn.
  • If the riser has to be touched, agree the shut-off time with the building manager and warn the neighbours above and below.
  • Clear access to the riser box, the hatch, the sink cupboard and the space behind the washing machine.
  • In a wooden house, show the way to the basement and the meter pit; in winter, clear the snow off the pit cover.
  • In the form, describe the pipe material and length and attach a photo.

What people usually ask

May I replace the pipes in my flat without asking the building?

The Apartment Ownership Law allows work in a flat without approval, as far as it does not affect common property. Pipes beyond your valves usually do not. If the first valve or the connection to the riser has to be replaced, the riser has to be shut off, and that is agreed with the building manager.

In a wooden house the pipe by the outer wall freezes every winter. What can be done?

The best option is to move the pipe further from the outer wall or keep the room warmer than 2 °C, as the building code requires. If that is not possible, the pipe is insulated without gaps, also at bends and valves. A frozen pipe is thawed slowly, with a warm cloth, never with a flame.

Who repairs the incoming pipe in the yard between the street and a wooden house?

The Rīgas ūdens network usually ends at the outer wall of the meter pit or at the plot boundary. The stretch from there to the house is the owner's responsibility, and is repaired by a plumber the owner orders. If it leaks on the street side, report it to Rīgas ūdens.

How much does replacing old pipes in a flat cost?

Pipes are from €10/m, and each new water point up to an appliance from €35. Chasing, restoring the finish and materials are extra. You talk through the total price on the call, once the length and the number of points are known.
