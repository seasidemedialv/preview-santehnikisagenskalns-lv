The riser runs through all floors and is the building's common property, so its replacement is usually decided by the flat owners' association, unless the building's documents say otherwise. Once a decision is made, the plumber replaces the section on your floor or the whole riser together with the neighbours.

When the old riser gives itself away

  • The steel pipe near the ceiling or floor has rust swellings, and a joint drips.
  • Brownish water and weak pressure are on all floors, not only yours.
  • A crack is visible in the cast-iron soil stack, a joint is damp or you can smell it in the box.
  • Renovation of the bathroom is starting, and the riser box will be opened anyway.
  • The neighbours above and below have already replaced their sections, and yours is the only old one.

In Soviet-era blocks of flats in Bolderāja, Daugavgrīva and Dzirciems, the risers are usually in the bathroom or toilet behind a tiled box, and next to them runs the hot water riser, to which a heated towel rail is connected.

How a riser is replaced

  1. Decision and approval For work that affects common property, the Apartment Ownership Law requires the consent of the flat owners' association. The building manager says whether the decision already exists, who organises the work and when the riser can be shut off.
  2. Neighbours and access While the riser is shut off, the whole vertical line is without water. If the whole riser is replaced, the plumber has to get into every flat; the law obliges owners to let in specialists authorised by the manager if the work is needed for the building's utilities to operate.
  3. Opening up The box or the tiles around the riser are opened wide enough to reach the points where it passes through the ceiling and floor. Belongings are carried out of the bathroom beforehand.
  4. Cutting out the old section and fitting the new one After the manager has shut off and drained the riser, the old section is cut out between floors and a new one is fitted, with transitions to the old pipe above and below. A branch for the flat, with its own valves, is built into the new section.
  5. Filling and testing The riser is filled, and the joints are tested under pressure. It is worth closing the box afterwards with a hatch to the valves and joints, so that next time the tiles do not have to be broken; restoring the box is not plumbing work.

Soil stack replacement: cast iron to plastic

A soil stack cannot be shut off with a valve. While a cast-iron section is out, the neighbours above must not use water, wash or flush the toilet, so the time is agreed with the whole vertical line. Cast-iron pipe is heavy and brittle; it is taken out in pieces, taking care that the old socket one floor below does not crack.

The new plastic section is joined to the old cast iron above and below with rubber transitions. The building code requires a cleanout at the bottom and top of the stack and in the sections in the basement, so if the old section had a cleaning opening, it is kept in the new one too. On the top floor the stack continues to the roof and ventilates the drains of the whole building.

LBN 221-15, clause 253 (cleanout at the bottom and top of the stack and in the basement) and clause 249 (the drains are ventilated through the stack above the roof). Checked 13.11.2026.

One floor or the whole building: price and what changes it

A water riser section on one flat's floor is from €110, a soil stack section from €100. Reconnecting the flat's branch to the new riser is separate: a water or drain point from €35. Prices are for labour only; materials are extra.

The price changes with opening the box, the pipe material, how many floors are replaced at once, and how easily the points through the ceiling and floor can be reached. The tiles and the riser boxing are restored afterwards by a finishing tradesperson; that is not plumbing work. If there is a travel charge, we tell you on the call before the work.

A new section between two old ones: what it means for the neighbours

If you replace only your own floor, you get a new pipe in the bathroom, but the transition to the old pipe stays in the ceiling and floor. If the old steel or cast iron above and below is weak, it can start to leak next to the new section, and then the neighbour suffers. If the whole vertical line is replaced at once, the transitions are only in the basement and on the top floor or in the attic, but all the flats are without water at the same time and all the boxes have to be opened.

The riser is the building's common property, and its maintenance and repair are the manager's task. How the costs are split if you replace a section on your own initiative is usually set by the building's decisions; ask the building manager. More on this in the guide who pays for riser replacement.

Apartment Ownership Law, Section 4 (common property), Section 10 (access for the manager's specialists), Section 11 (work affecting common property needs the association's consent). Residential Property Management Law, Section 6. Checked 16.11.2026.

What people usually ask

The neighbour above does not agree to the riser replacement. What should I do?

The riser is common property, so its replacement is decided by the flat owners' association, not by one neighbour. If the work is needed for the building's utilities to operate, the law obliges an owner to let in specialists authorised by the manager. To find out how to achieve that in a particular building, ask the building manager.

Can I replace only my own floor while the building waits for a joint renovation?

Often you can, if the association agrees (the manager usually arranges this) and the manager shuts off the riser. Note that the transitions to the old pipe stay in the ceiling and floor; if the old steel is very weak, it is worth talking to the neighbours about replacing several floors at once.

After the replacement, will I have to restore the tiles around the riser?

If the riser is behind a tiled box, it is opened, and tiling is separate work that is not part of the riser price. It is worth closing the box with a hatch to the valves and joints.

Who pays if the riser is replaced in my flat?

The riser is the building's common property, so the building usually decides on it and funds it, unless the building's documents say otherwise. If you replace a section on your own initiative, ask the building manager how the costs are split.
