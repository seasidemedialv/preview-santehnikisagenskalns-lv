Replacing a bath with a shower - in a small Soviet-era bathroom

On the plumbing side, the bath and its connections are removed, the outlet is prepared for the shower and a cabin, tray or drain is installed. The price of this part is set after inspection; a shower cabin in a prepared spot is from €125. Waterproofing and tiling are another trade.

When a shower in place of a bath is a sensible choice

  • Almost nobody bathes in the bath, but the shower is used every day.
  • Stepping over the edge of the bath has become hard.
  • The enamel of the old cast-iron bath is worn, and the bath has to be replaced anyway.
  • You need room for a washing machine, which has nowhere else to go in the bathroom.
  • A bathroom renovation with new tiles is starting, and the connections can be moved now.

The order: from the old bath to a shower

  1. The option You choose a shower cabin, a tray with a glass wall or a shower area with a drain in the floor. That determines where and at what height the outlet is needed.
  2. Disconnecting The water is shut off, and the bath mixer tap, the waste and the overflow are disconnected.
  3. Removing the bath The bath is taken out. In Soviet-era bathrooms it can be built in under the tiles or have a brick screen, so the surrounding tiles suffer.
  4. Checking the outlet The bath branch and the tee to the riser are inspected. If it is cast iron and cracked, it is replaced now, while the floor is open.
  5. Water and outlets for the shower The outlet and the mixer tap supplies are moved or lowered according to the chosen option.
  6. The tiler's turn Waterproofing and tiles are laid by another tradesperson, after the plumbing is prepared.
  7. Installing the shower The cabin or tray is installed, the trap and the water are connected, and everything is tested with water.

Removing and replacing a cast-iron bath

A cast-iron bath is heavy, and it is usually carried out by several people; in a building without a lift it goes down the stairs. Removal and carrying out is one of the factors that set the price of the plumbing part. On the call, also talk through where the bath will go after it is carried out.

If you do not want to replace the bath with a shower but only to swap it, installing a steel or acrylic bath is from €60. Installing a cast-iron bath, removing the old one and the screen are not included in this price.

Outlet height decides whether a tray is enough

The bath waste is low by the floor, and its outlet usually runs to the riser almost at floor level. A tray trap needs height under the tray, and a floor drain needs even more room. For a shower waste pipe, LBN 221-15 sets a minimum diameter of 40 mm, and for pipes up to 50 mm a fall of 30 ‰ is needed, which is 3 cm per metre.

In the estates built in the 1970s and 80s in Zasulauks and Dzirciems, as in other blocks of flats, the floor slab between storeys in a flat usually cannot be deepened. So either a taller tray is chosen or the floor in the shower area is raised. If the shower has to be level with the floor, the threshold at the door gets higher.

Waterproofing is another trade

A shower in place of a bath means water splashes onto the floor and walls, where the edge of the bath used to hold it back. Waterproofing under the tiles is the tiler's work, and it is not in this price. The plumbing work comes both before and after the tiler: first the outlets and the drain body, then the equipment.

In a block of flats, a mistake in the waterproofing or in the drain connection goes to the neighbour below, and it may be noticed only months later. Before tiling it is worth testing the outlets with water.

What the plumbing part of the price is made of

  • removing and carrying out the old bath;
  • a shower cabin, a shower tray or a shower area with a drain;
  • whether the sewer outlet has to be moved or lowered because of the slope;
  • whether the water outlets and the mixer tap have to be moved;
  • the floor level and access to the riser.

Separate items: installing a shower cabin is from €125, a water or drain outlet from €35 per point. Tiling and waterproofing are not included. Labour only; materials are extra.

What people usually ask

Does replacing a bath with a shower have to be agreed with the building manager?

The Apartment Ownership Law allows work inside a flat without approval, as long as it does not affect common property. If the riser has to be touched or the water shut off for the whole building, you cannot do without the building manager. For your own building, ask the building manager.

Do the sewer pipes also have to be replaced after the bath is removed?

Not always. If the old cast-iron branch is sound and at the right height, it can stay. If it is cracked or has to be lowered, see waste pipe replacement.

Will there be room for a washing machine next to the shower?

That is one of the reasons a bath is replaced with a shower. A machine needs a water tap and an outlet, and it is practical to put them in now, while the walls and the floor are open. For the connection, see the page on washing machine connection.
