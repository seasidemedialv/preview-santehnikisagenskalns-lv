In Soviet-era toilet rooms the toilet often has an angled outlet to the riser socket at the floor, while many new toilets have a straight (horizontal) outlet into the wall, and a suitable adapter joins the two. Installing or replacing a floor toilet is from €50, and removing the old toilet is usually +€10–35.

When it is better to replace a toilet than repair it

  • The toilet or the cistern has a crack that water seeps through.
  • Water keeps appearing at the base even though the joint has already been resealed.
  • The flushing mechanism can no longer be found for the old model.
  • The toilet rocks because the floor fixings have crumbled.
  • The toilet room is being renovated, and the old toilet does not go with the new tiles or the frame.

From the old socket to a new toilet

  1. Water and the old toilet The water to the cistern is shut off, the cistern is emptied and the old toilet is removed. Old toilets are often cemented in place, so removal is a separate job.
  2. Checking the socket The socket on the riser or branch is cleaned and inspected. In a cast-iron socket the plumber looks for cracks and remains of old sealing, because the new adapter holds only on a clean and sound surface.
  3. Adapter to suit the outlet An angled outlet into a low socket and a straight outlet into the wall need different adapters. For a toilet waste pipe, LBN 221-15 sets a minimum connection diameter of 100 mm, so the adapter does not narrow it.
  4. Positioning and fixing The toilet is set by spirit level and bolted to the floor. In a small toilet room it is also checked that the door closes and that the valve beside the cistern can be reached.
  5. Water and testing The cistern is connected to an angle valve with a flexible hose and flushed several times, checking that the outlet and the supply stay dry. The base is sealed with sanitary silicone; Sika states that its silicone cures about 3 mm deep in 24 hours.

Installing a wall-hung toilet in a small toilet room

Installing a wall-hung toilet consists of two jobs. First the frame (the installation) with the cistern is set up and connected to the water and the drains. Then comes the plasterboard cladding and tiling, and only on the finished wall is the toilet hung. Fitting a frame with a wall-hung toilet is from €85 per set; cladding and tiling are extra.

The frame rests on the floor and is fixed to the wall. Geberit states that the Duofix frame has been tested for loads of up to 400 kg, but that applies to the frame, not to the wall it is fixed to. In a narrow Soviet-era toilet room the frame takes up part of the depth of the room, and the riser is often right there in the corner. The connection to the riser is finished and tested before the cladding, because it can no longer be reached afterwards.

The mechanism is reached later by removing the button panel, so the tiles do not have to be broken.

Labour price for a toilet and a frame

A floor toilet is from €50, a wall-hung toilet on a frame from €85, and removing the old toilet is usually +€10–35. The price goes up with a cracked cast-iron socket, a non-standard adapter, a new valve before the cistern or the need to move the outlet. If the toilet only leaks but is sound, a cistern repair from €35 is cheaper. Labour only; materials are extra. If there is a travel charge, it is stated on the call before the work.

An outlet above the neighbour's ceiling

The toilet's connection to the riser is in the floor slab or directly above it, and the neighbour's toilet is below it. A poorly sealed adapter rarely leaks in a stream; more often it seeps a drop at a time with every flush. The damp soaks into the slab, and a stain on the neighbour's ceiling appears later. So after the work it is worth watching the floor by the toilet for a few days and asking the neighbour below to do the same.

In Āgenskalna priedes, the first large Soviet-era housing estate in Riga (1958–1962), the toilet rooms are narrow, and the riser stands next to the toilet. If the building is preparing to replace its risers, it is better to replace the toilet after the new sewer tee is in. For that, read the page on riser replacement in a flat.

What people usually ask

Will a new toilet fit the old cast-iron socket?

Usually yes, with a suitable adapter, if the socket is sound. If it has a crack or is crumbling, the branch or the socket has to be replaced, and that is pipe work with its own price.

Can a toilet be replaced without shutting off the water to the neighbours?

Yes, if the cistern has its own angle valve or the flat's valves close. If neither holds, the riser has to be shut off, and that is done by the building manager or someone they appoint.

Which is easier to clean and repair: a floor or a wall-hung toilet?

The floor is easier to wash under a wall-hung toilet, but its cistern is behind the wall, and the mechanism is changed through the button opening. With a floor toilet everything is visible and accessible. The choice depends on whether the toilet room is being renovated with a new wall anyway.
