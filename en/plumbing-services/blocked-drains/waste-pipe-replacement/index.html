These pipes are part of your flat, so they can be replaced without a decision of the flat owners' association, but the connection to the soil stack has to be made with as little contact with the shared pipe as possible. The work is from €30 per point.

A waste pipe leaks or smells: signs under the bath and by the toilet

  • A damp patch under the bath or by the toilet appears only when water is let out.
  • A drain smell comes from the floor or the box by the riser, even though the traps are full.
  • The water drains slowly and blocks again and again, because the inside of the old cast iron is rough and overgrown.
  • A crack or a layer of rust is visible on the cast-iron pipe, and the joints are sealed with old cement.
  • Renovation of the bathroom is starting, and the old pipes will be visible anyway.

If a waste pipe leaks, closing the valves does not help: drains are not under pressure, and they leak only when somebody uses water. Until the repair, do not use the appliance that leaks, and warn the neighbour below.

Replacing cast-iron drains step by step

  1. Disconnecting the appliances The traps are removed, the washing machine is disconnected and, if needed, the toilet. The neighbours above are asked not to use water for a while, while the riser socket is open.
  2. Taking out the old cast iron The cast-iron pipe is divided into pieces and taken out of the riser socket carefully, so that the socket itself does not crack. If it cracks, the job turns into a riser section, and the building manager has to be told.
  3. Connection to the riser A rubber adapter is placed in the old cast-iron socket, and the new plastic pipe goes into it. If the cast-iron tee is damaged, it is replaced together with a riser section.
  4. New pipes with a fall The pipes are laid with an even fall towards the riser: for thin pipes up to 50 mm, 30 ‰ or 3 cm per metre; for thicker ones (75–100 mm), 20 ‰ or 2 cm per metre. Between connections the fall does not change, and it must not exceed 150 ‰.
  5. Traps and a test with water Each appliance has its own trap; appliances in different rooms are not connected to one trap. All appliances are run in turn with water and the joints are inspected.

The diameter is set by the appliance. The building code requires at least 100 mm for a toilet, 32 mm is enough for a small hand basin, while a bath, shower, kitchen sink and both machines (washing machine and dishwasher) need 40 mm.

LBN 221-15 (Cabinet Regulations No. 332): falls in clauses 232, 269 and 272; traps in clauses 213 and 216; minimum diameters in Annex 3. Checked 13.11.2026.

Starting prices: a point, a riser, a new outlet

Replacing a horizontal drain up to one appliance (a sink, bath, toilet or washing machine) is from €30 per point. If a section of the cast-iron riser on your floor also has to be replaced, that is from €100, and it is agreed with the building manager. A new outlet in a place where there was none before is from €35.

The price goes up with a bath or toilet that has to be taken off, pipes under tiles and in the floor, and a tee in the riser whose old socket is weak. This is the labour price, materials are extra. If there is a travel charge, we tell you on the call before the work.

Noise, floor height and fall in a small bathroom

Cast iron is heavy and muffles the sound of water better than plastic. After replacement the noise of water in the pipes may be more audible, especially from the neighbours above, because the riser stays the same. A pipe lining or a box with sound insulation reduces it.

A fall needs height. In a Soviet-era bathroom where the bath stands against one wall and the riser is in the opposite corner, a 40 mm pipe over three metres with a 3 cm fall per metre drops by 9 cm. If the outlet in the floor is too high, the bath is raised on legs or the shower tray on a base. More on this on the page replacing a bath with a shower.

Only the riser is common property

The Apartment Ownership Law counts the utilities up to the common-property risers as part of the flat, and work in the flat may be done without approval as far as it does not affect common property. So you can replace the pipes from the appliances to the riser yourselves, but work on the riser itself or its tee is agreed with the building manager.

Apartment Ownership Law, Sections 3 and 11. Checked 16.11.2026.

What people usually ask

Can only the cast-iron piece from the toilet to the riser be replaced?

Yes, that is one point, from €30. For a toilet connection the building code provides at least 100 mm diameter, and the connection to the old cast-iron socket is made with a rubber collar. If the toilet has to be taken off and put back, the price is higher.

After plastic pipes, will we hear the neighbours' water more?

You may hear more, because plastic muffles sound worse than cast iron. Most of the noise still comes from the riser, which stays unchanged. A box with sound insulation or a pipe lining reduces the noise.

What if the old riser socket cracks during the work?

Then the riser section on your floor has to be replaced, from €100. It is common property, so the building manager is told at once, and the neighbours above do not use water until the repair is finished.
