If water stands in one sink, bath or toilet but the neighbours' drains run fine, the fault is usually in the trap or the pipe up to the soil stack, and the plumber clears it. In an old wooden house the blockage can also be in the yard line between the house and the first inspection chamber.

Your branch, the shared soil stack and the basement horizontal drain

In a block of flats each flat has its own branch: the pipes from the sink, bath, washing machine and toilet to the connection on the soil stack. The soil stack runs through all floors, in the basement it turns into a horizontal drain, and that drain leads to the street network. The soil stack and the horizontal drain serve the whole building, so their cleaning is usually organised by the building manager.

The sign of where to look is simple. If water stands only in your appliances, your branch is blocked. If water let out by the neighbour above appears in your bath or shower tray, the blockage is in the soil stack below your floor or in the basement drain. Then do not use water, contact the building manager and warn the neighbours above, because everything they flush reaches you.

Under the building code, a building's internal drainage runs from the appliances to the first inspection chamber outside the building. Where the building's network ends and the water utility's network begins is set by the ownership boundary, so for a blockage on the street side ask the building manager or Rīgas ūdens.

Building Code LBN 221-15, clause 5 (Cabinet Regulations No. 332 of 30.06.2015): internal drainage runs to the first inspection chamber outside the building. The ownership boundary between networks is set by the Water Management Services Law. Checked 13.11.2026.

A wooden house's yard line to the city chamber

In an old wooden house in Āgenskalns, Torņakalns or Zasulauks the drain leaves the house under the floor and runs across the yard to a chamber where the city network begins. If this stretch blocks, water stops in all appliances, and the first to show it is usually the toilet or a floor drain at the lowest point. If the house has several flats, the yard line is shared, and the owners or the building manager agree on its cleaning.

The yard line is cleaned from the chamber or cleanout side with a motorised drain snake. A rotating spiral breaks up grease deposits and cuts roots that have grown into joints. The building code requires a chamber at every place where an external line turns, changes its fall or diameter, or where another line joins it, and on a straight run of pipe thinner than 200 mm a chamber at least every 35 m. So a yard line can usually be reached in more than one place.

Building Code LBN 223-15, clause 73 (Cabinet Regulations No. 327 of 30.06.2015): chambers at joins and changes of direction and every 35 m for pipes up to 200 mm. Checked 13.11.2026.

When jetting helps, not a drain snake

A snake bores a way through the blockage, but the grease that has built up on the pipe walls for years stays. A high-pressure water jet, used in jetting, washes this layer off the walls and is effective precisely for small-diameter pipes.

In a flat, jetting is chosen if a kitchen or bath branch blocks repeatedly. In the yard line of a wooden or private house, it is chosen if the line soon starts to run slowly again after a snake. Jetting a soil stack and a basement horizontal drain in a block of flats is usually ordered by the building manager. Rīgas ūdens advises putting grease, oil and coffee grounds in the rubbish and keeping a strainer in the sink, so that the branch blocks less often.

A drain smell in a bathroom that is rarely used

A trap holds a water seal that stops sewer air entering the room; the building rules require it to keep a depth of at least 25 mm. When an appliance stands unused for a long while, the trap's water dries out and the smell appears. It is often a guest shower or a floor drain.

  • First pour water into every trap and drain. If the smell disappears, the fault was an empty seal.
  • If the smell stays, it can come from a loose connection to the soil stack, a cracked piece of cast iron or a washing machine outlet with no trap.
  • If a trap gurgles when the neighbour above flushes the toilet, the soil stack's ventilation may not be working; that is a matter for the building manager.

A soil stack is ventilated through the roof. In buildings of up to five floors, a drain vacuum valve may be fitted at the end of an unventilated stack if the building has at least one ventilated stack, and the valve must sit above the top edge of the highest appliance. If a cast-iron pipe in the flat has cracked, it is replaced: see waste pipe replacement.

LBN 221-15, clause 280 (vacuum valve in buildings of up to 5 floors); a water seal of at least 25 mm, for example in the UK building regulations guidance (Approved Document H). Checked 13.11.2026.

A cleanout hatch behind tiles: how to reach the soil stack

A cleanout is a point on the pipe with a removable cover, through which the snake is fed. The building code provides them at the bottom and top of soil stacks, on stack sections in the basement, at bends, and at the head of any branch that serves three or more appliances. Inspection chambers in domestic drainage inside a building are prohibited.

In Soviet-era series blocks the soil stack in the bathroom is often boxed in with plasterboard and tiles. If there is no hatch at the cleanout, the box has to be opened for cleaning or inspecting the stack. When a box is built anew or a bathroom is re-tiled, a cleanout hatch at the cleanout point lets you reach it next time without breaking tiles.

LBN 221-15, clause 253 (cleanout locations) and clause 258 (inspection chambers inside a building prohibited). Checked 13.11.2026.

Before the plumber comes: the way to the trap, the hatch and the chamber

  • Clear the space under the sink and by the bath panel, and put towels or a bucket by the toilet.
  • Note which appliances do not drain and whether the neighbours above or below have the same.
  • If the water comes up from the soil stack, contact the building manager and note down the manager's contact details.
  • In a wooden or private house, find the yard chamber and clear snow, leaves or belongings off its cover.

What people usually ask

The neighbour's water appears in my bath. Is my pipe at fault?

Usually not. If water from the upper floors comes up in your appliances, the shared soil stack below your floor or the basement drain is blocked. Do not use water, warn the neighbours above and contact the building manager.

May I call a plumber myself to clean the soil stack, without the building manager?

The soil stack serves the whole building, so its cleaning is usually decided by the building manager. Ask the manager: if they agree that you order the work, the plumber can also clean the soil stack from your flat's cleanout.

How much does clearing a blockage in a flat cost?

Clearing a blockage in the internal drains, toilet or kitchen sink is from €50, high-pressure jetting from €60 per hour. This is the labour price, materials are extra; you talk through the final price on the call after you describe the situation.
