Radiator replacement - a new radiator in place of cast-iron sections

Replacing radiators in a block of flats in Pārdaugava, Riga's left bank, is a two-step job: first the approval and shutting off the riser with the building manager, then the radiator itself.

The old cast-iron sections are removed, a steel or bimetal radiator is fixed to the wall and connected to the same supply pipes. In a private house with its own boiler there is nobody to get approval from, and the system is drained and refilled on site, without the building manager.

Signs the old radiator has to be replaced

  • Damp streaks or rust runs appear between the cast-iron sections: the section seals have dried out.
  • The valve at the radiator no longer turns, and the radiator cannot be shut off.
  • The radiator stays cold at the bottom even after bleeding, and sludge has built up in it.
  • In a bathroom or room renovation you want the radiator somewhere else or swapped for a smaller one.
  • There is so much rust on the threads and the body that every movement risks a leak.

From approval to a warm radiator

  1. Approval from the building manager In a block of flats, radiator replacement must be approved, because the riser up to the first valve in the flat is common property. How to do it is explained in the guide replacing radiators in a flat and approval.
  2. Shutting off and draining the riser The riser is shut off by the building manager or someone they authorise. In a private house, the system is shut off at the boiler and drained through the drain tap.
  3. Removing the old radiator The supply pipes are unscrewed or cut, and the cast-iron radiator is lifted off its hooks. It is heavy, so a large radiator is sometimes taken apart by sections in the flat.
  4. The new radiator, valves and bypass Brackets are fixed to the wall, the radiator is hung and valves are fitted on the supply pipes. If the connection had a bypass pipe, it is kept.
  5. Filling and bleeding When the riser or the system is filled, the radiator is bled and every connection is inspected until it warms evenly.

A steel panel or bimetal radiator on the building's riser

The size and output of the new radiator are chosen close to the old one. Without a calculation you may not significantly increase the radiator's output or change the connection scheme, because it takes heat from the neighbours on the same riser. Whether a steel panel or bimetal radiator suits the building's system, ask the building manager during the approval: many buildings have their own requirements.

The bypass pipe under the radiator: why it is not cut out

In many Soviet-era buildings the heating is a single-pipe system: water goes along the riser from floor to floor, and the radiator is connected on the side. A short pipe between the radiator's supply pipes, a bypass, lets water keep flowing up the riser even when the radiator is shut off. If the bypass is cut out and valves are fitted, the neighbours below you can be left without heat as soon as you turn off your radiator.

So when a radiator is replaced, the bypass is kept or fitted anew, and the valves go on the radiator's supply pipes, not on the riser. Next time the radiator has to be removed, it can be shut off with its own valves, without draining the riser.

Moving a radiator to another wall

Moving a radiator under another window or to another wall means new supply pipes from the riser and a different connection scheme. In a block of flats it has to be approved in the same way as a replacement, and if the building manager asks, a calculation is needed. In Soviet-era buildings the pipes often pass through the floor slab into the neighbour's flat, so the riser's position usually cannot be moved within one flat.

In a private house moving is simpler: the supply pipes are extended along the wall or in the floor, and the system is bled afterwards. Longer supply pipes also mean more metres of pipe and more work.

How much radiator replacement costs and what makes it dearer

Installing or replacing a radiator is from €75 per radiator: the old one is removed, the new one is mounted and connected to the same supply pipes, filled and bled. The work on new heating supply pipes is priced after inspection. Shutting off the riser through the building manager in a block of flats is extra.

The price goes up with a heavy cast-iron radiator that has to be taken apart and carried down the stairs, rusted threads that have to be cut, new valves and a bypass, and moving. This is labour only; materials are extra. If there is a travel charge, it is stated on the call before the work.

What happens to the neighbours while the riser is empty

While the riser is shut off, the radiators on it are cold on every floor, so warn the neighbours and agree on a day. When the riser is filled, the water comes under pressure, and a weakly tightened connection can start to leak at your place or at the neighbour's below. At that moment someone has to be at the radiator to see it.

If the old supply pipe is so rusted that the thread breaks, the job reaches a section of the riser, and that is common property. In such a case, talk to the building manager before the work.

What people usually ask

Can a cast-iron radiator be replaced if the neighbours do not agree to the riser being shut off?

The riser is shut off by the building manager, who also reaches agreement with the building. Your task is to get the replacement approved and to warn the neighbours. If there is a dispute in the building, ask the building manager how it is to be resolved.

May the new radiator be bigger than the old one?

In a block of flats you may not significantly increase the output without a calculation, because the heat is taken from the neighbours. Choose a size close to the old one or ask the building manager.

Can I replace just one radiator out of three?

Yes. Each radiator is on its own supply pipe, and only the one that needs it can be replaced. If they are all on one riser, it makes sense to replace them together, because the riser is shut off once.

Does radiator replacement in a private house in Bieriņi have to be approved?

If the house is yours and the heating is only yours, there is nobody to get approval from. After the replacement the system is bled and, if needed, the pressure is topped up.
