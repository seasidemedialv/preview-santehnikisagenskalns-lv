Radiator repair - a leak, air and a thermostatic head

Radiator repair in Āgenskalns, Riga most often means one of three things: the radiator drips at a connection, there is air in it or it cannot be adjusted.

A leak at the valve or the supply pipe is fixed by replacing a seal, a connection or a short section, air is let out through the bleed valve, and for adjustment a thermostatic head is fitted on a thermostatic valve. If the job touches the riser, in a block of flats you first have to talk to the building manager.

What the radiator shows when it needs a plumber

  • There are drops under the valve or the nut, and a damp patch on the floor under the windowsill.
  • Water seeps out between the cast-iron sections and leaves a brown streak.
  • The radiator is cool at the top and warm at the bottom, and you can hear bubbling.
  • The radiator is cold at the bottom and warm at the top even after bleeding.
  • The valve or the thermostatic head does not turn, and the radiator heats all the time or not at all.

A leaking radiator: what to do while you wait for the plumber

A radiator leak rarely starts fast: usually it is drops at the valve stem, at the nut on the supply pipe or between the sections. Put down a container and a towel, wipe it and watch where the first drop comes from. If the radiator has two valves and they turn, close both: the radiator is cut off from the riser, and the leak will stop.

If there are no valves or they do not close, the heating water comes from the whole building's system, and then contact the building manager. Warn the neighbour below, because hot heating water reaches their ceiling through the slab. In a private house a leak slowly lowers the pressure, and you can see it on the boiler's pressure gauge.

How the plumber fixes a leak and air

  1. The leak position The connections are wiped and dried, and the plumber watches where the water appears first: the stem, the nut, the thread, the gap between sections or the body itself.
  2. Isolating the radiator If the radiator has its own valves, it is shut off and the water is drained from it alone. If not, the riser is shut off by the building manager, and in a private house the system is shut off at the boiler.
  3. The repair The valve stem gets a new seal or the whole valve is replaced, the nut gets a new seal and a rusted thread gets a short section of pipe. If water seeps from between the sections or the body is through, replacing the radiator is considered.
  4. Filling and bleeding The radiator or the riser is filled and bled. In a private house the pressure on the boiler's gauge is checked afterwards.
  5. Testing when hot When the radiator warms up again, the connections are inspected once more: some leaks appear only when the metal expands.

Air in a radiator: bleeding and when it is not enough

When the top edge of a radiator is cooler than the bottom, air has usually collected inside. It is let out like this: the heating is switched off, the radiator is allowed to cool, the bleed valve is opened with a key until the hissing stops and water runs evenly, and the valve is closed. In a private house the pressure usually drops afterwards, and the system has to be topped up.

If air keeps coming back, something in the system leaks or it is being topped up with water that contains air, and the cause has to be found. If the radiator is cold at the bottom and warm at the top, bleeding will not help: there is usually sludge. How to tell the difference yourself is in the guide why a radiator stays cold.

The bleeding order, the pressure drop and the sign of sludge: Worcester Bosch, How to bleed a radiator. Checked on 16.11.2026.

Fitting a thermostatic head on an old radiator

A thermostatic head works only on a thermostatic valve body. If the radiator has an old ball valve or nothing at all, the valve body has to be replaced first, and for that the radiator has to be cut off from the riser. If a thermostatic valve is already there, the head is screwed on without letting the water out, even in the heating season.

In a single-pipe system, which is common in Soviet-era buildings, a thermostatic valve suits only if there is a bypass pipe under the radiator. Without it, closing the radiator with the valve would stop the water for the whole riser. Replacing a valve body affects a heating element whose operation depends on the building's system, so in a block of flats it is approved by the building manager.

Heating elements in a flat whose operation depends on common property utilities are common property (Apartment Ownership Law, Section 4); the connection scheme may not be changed without a calculation (LV portāls e-consultation). Checked on 16.11.2026.

The price of the repair and what it depends on

Fixing a leak at a radiator is from €40: the plumber finds where it leaks and, where it is accessible, replaces a seal, a connection or a short piece of the supply pipe. When the cause is not known, the plumber's visit and inspection is from €40, and longer work is charged by the hour, from €25/h. Bleeding has no fixed price: it depends on the number of radiators, whether pressure has to be topped up in a private house and whether the air returns.

The price goes up with shutting off the riser through the building manager, rusted threads and whether the radiator has to be taken off the wall. This is labour only; materials are extra. If there is a travel charge, it is stated on the call before the work.

When the repair reaches the riser and the neighbours

The supply pipe from the riser to the radiator is short, and in an old building its thread at the riser can be as rusted as the valve itself. If the thread breaks when it is turned, the repair moves to the riser, and that is the building's common property. So before replacing a valve in a block of flats it is worth knowing the building manager's contact.

What people usually ask

The radiator drips only at night. Can it wait until summer?

A small leak at the valve can be watched by putting a container under it, but it usually grows, and water reaches the neighbour's ceiling. If the radiator can be shut off with its own valves, the repair can also be done during the heating season.

Can a thermostatic head be fitted without shutting off the riser?

Yes, if the radiator already has a thermostatic valve: the head is screwed on without letting the water out. If there is no valve, the radiator has to be cut off to change the body, and in a block of flats that is done with the building manager's knowledge.

Is it worth repairing a cast-iron radiator that seeps between the sections?

The gap between sections can be resealed, but for that the radiator has to be removed and taken apart. Whether it pays off against radiator replacement, weigh it up on the call.
