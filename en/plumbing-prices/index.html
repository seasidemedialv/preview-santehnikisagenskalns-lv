Plumber by the hour: when you pay for time, not for a job

The hourly rate is from €25/h; a call-out is usually billed for at least one hour. The hourly rate suits a visit with several small jobs that have no row of their own in the table: tightening a leaking joint under a sink, replacing a washer in a tap, checking a cistern. How much a plumber costs per hour in your case depends on the list of jobs, so list them all in the form. For this kind of visit there is a page on a plumber for small jobs.

What pushes the total above the starting price

  • Access: a pipe in a wall or under tiles needs chasing and restoring the finish, and these are separate costs.
  • Riser: where a radiator or heated towel rail sits on a shared riser, the building manager shuts it off and refills it, and that is not part of our price.
  • Old parts: removing a cast-iron bath, an old toilet or an old water heater is usually a separate row or a surcharge.
  • Materials: pipes, fittings, flexible hoses, traps and spare parts are not in the labour price.
  • Floor level and slope: if the drain outlet has to be lowered or moved, the price goes up.

If there is a travel charge, it is named on the call before the work.

What people usually ask

Do I pay the call-out if the plumber does the job on the same visit?

Usually not. The call-out row (from €40) covers the trip and the inspection, and when the plumber starts the job right there, this amount is usually included in the labour price.

Why do some rows say “after inspection” instead of a price?

There the amount depends on things that can only be seen on site: the length of the line, the faulty part, the floor level or the size of the system. The factors are written in the table next to the job.

Does the radiator price include shutting off the riser?

No. In a block of flats the building manager shuts off the riser, and that is a separate matter. The radiator row includes removing the old one, connecting the new one, refilling and bleeding.
