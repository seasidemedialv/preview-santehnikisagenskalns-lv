Who pays for riser replacement - the law, the building manager and your floor

Who pays for riser replacement in Āgenskalns and elsewhere in Pārdaugava, Riga, is usually set by the building's documents and the decisions of the flat owners' association, not by the plumber. The riser is the common property of the whole building, even if it runs through your bathroom, and maintaining it is the building manager's job. Your part starts at the branch off the riser.

Where the building's pipe ends in your bathroom

Under Section 3 of the Apartment Ownership Law, the flat includes its utilities up to the shared risers, and also the bath, shower and toilet with their fittings. Section 4 counts the building's utility systems as common property. So the vertical pipe that runs from the basement to the roof belongs to all the owners, while the branch from it to your sink is yours.

In practice the boundary is usually the first valve of the flat on the branch: up to it the pipe is common property, and beyond it the flat's network begins. For drains it is usually the place where your line joins the riser, unless the building's documents say otherwise. In a Soviet-era bathroom this spot is often behind a tiled box in the corner, so before a renovation it is worth knowing exactly where it is.

Apartment Ownership Law, Sections 3 and 4 (likumi.lv); the LV portāls e-consultation on the riser up to the first flat valve. Checked on 16.11.2026.

Who decides when risers are replaced in the whole building

Under Section 6 of the Residential Property Management Law, the building manager must ensure water and drainage and must inspect, maintain and keep the building's utilities in routine repair. If a riser leaks or is blocked, it is reported to the building manager, who organises the work.

A larger replacement on all floors is a common-property job, decided by the flat owners' association. How the costs are divided between the flats and from what funds they are paid is usually set by the management agreement and the association's decision. Unless the building's documents say otherwise, this question is settled by the whole building together, not by one owner with a plumber.

Residential Property Management Law, Section 6; Apartment Ownership Law, Section 11 (likumi.lv). Checked on 16.11.2026.

Five questions for the building manager before anyone starts cutting a pipe

  1. Who is the building manager The name is usually on the maintenance bill. Also write down their emergency contact, because it will be useful when a riser leaks.
  2. Whether a replacement is already planned Ask whether the association has already decided about the risers. If the building will replace them in a year, there is no point in fitting a new section on your floor separately.
  3. Who covers the costs in your case Ask for the answer in writing: whether the work is the building's routine repair or your own initiative. Who pays usually depends on that.
  4. Who will shut off the riser The riser valves in the basement are common property. They are turned off by the building manager or a person they allow to, and opened again after the work.
  5. Whether access through the neighbours is needed Find out whether the joints will be on your floor or whether the work also has to go through the neighbour above or below, and who will warn them.

If you want to replace the riser only on your floor

This often happens during a bathroom renovation: the old steel water pipe or cast-iron drain will stay behind new tiles, and the owner wants to replace it now. Section 11 of the Apartment Ownership Law allows work in your flat without approval only as far as the work does not affect common property. A riser does affect it, so the association's consent is needed, and in practice it is arranged through the building manager.

The plumber joins a one-floor section to the old pipe above and below. If you, not the building, propose the replacement, ask the building manager who covers the costs: this is set by the building's documents and the association's decision. Before the work also agree where the cleanout hatch will be, so that the joint can be reached later without breaking the tiles.

When the plumber has to reach the neighbour above or below

A riser runs in one vertical line through all the floors. In the Āgenskalna priedes buildings, built in 1958–1962, and in later Soviet-era blocks of flats in Dzirciems or Bolderāja, all the flats in one vertical line are connected to the same riser. If the whole riser is replaced, the work takes place in each of them.

Section 10 of the Apartment Ownership Law says that an owner must allow the specialists authorised by the building manager to carry out in the flat the work needed for the building's utilities to function. So a neighbour who disagrees usually cannot stop the replacement of the whole building's riser, but the building manager agrees the time with them.

Apartment Ownership Law, Section 10 (likumi.lv). Checked on 16.11.2026.

What not to do yourself with a shared riser

  • Do not turn off the riser valve in the basement without the building manager knowing: the whole vertical line is left without water.
  • Do not start cutting a riser without written approval, even if the old pipe looks bad.
  • Do not tile a riser in tightly without a hatch: a soil stack needs a cleanout for cleaning.
  • Do not hide a leak behind new finishes; report it to the building manager while the damage is small.
  • Do not assume that the building pays for everything or that you pay for everything before the building manager has confirmed it.

When you need a plumber and what the labour costs

You need a plumber when the riser section on your floor has to be replaced in an agreed renovation or when the leak is in the joint between your branch and the riser. You also need one when the building manager allows the work and you look for the plumber yourself. Replacing one floor section of a water riser is from €110, and of a soil stack from €100. A call-out with inspection is from €40. This is the labour price; materials are extra.

The price changes with the pipe material (steel, cast iron, plastic), with whether the riser is behind tiles or in a box, and with access on the neighbours' floors. If there is a travel charge, it is named on the call before the work. How the work goes step by step is described on the page riser replacement in a flat, and all the prices are in the section prices.

What people usually ask

Can the building manager demand that I pay for the riser in my bathroom?

Under the Apartment Ownership Law the riser is common property, so the whole building usually decides about it. If you propose the replacement because of a renovation, the costs may be yours, unless the building's documents say otherwise. Ask the building manager for the answer in writing.

Is a soil stack common property in the same way as a water riser?

The law counts the building's utility systems as a whole as common property, so usually yes. Yours is the horizontal line from the sink, bath and toilet to the joint with the riser.

The riser is leaking on my floor, but the neighbour below is wet. Whom should I tell?

The building manager, because a leak in a riser is a fault in the building's utilities. Warn the neighbour, photograph the place and, if it leaks heavily, ask for the riser to be shut off. If your own branch behind the flat valve is leaking, turn it off yourself.

Can I refuse to let the plumbers in when the risers of the whole building are replaced?

Section 10 of the Apartment Ownership Law requires you to allow the specialists authorised by the building manager to carry out the work needed for the building's utilities. The time and access can usually be agreed with the building manager.
