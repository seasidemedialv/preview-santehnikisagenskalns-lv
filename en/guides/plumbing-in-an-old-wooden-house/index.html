Plumbing in an old wooden house - Āgenskalns, Torņakalns, Ķīpsala

Plumbing in an old wooden house in Āgenskalns, Torņakalns, Ķīpsala or Zasulauks differs from a brick building in three places. Pipes by the outer wall can freeze in winter, the incoming pipe under the yard is the house owner's responsibility right up to the boundary of the Rīgas ūdens network, and a light partition does not always have the strength for a full water heater. If the house is in a protected area, work on the outside is assessed before it starts.

Which wooden houses in Pārdaugava, Riga's left bank, stand in a protected area

The historic part of Āgenskalns, with its winding street network between Nometņu iela and Eduarda Smiļģa iela, is an urban planning monument of national importance, known for its wooden architecture. The low-rise buildings of the northern part of Ķīpsala from the turn of the 19th and 20th centuries have the same status, and Ķīpsala lies in the buffer zone of the UNESCO World Heritage site “Historic Centre of Riga”.

Torņakalns and Zasulauks also have many old wooden houses: after the fire of 1812, around 1,000 low-rise detached wooden houses were built in Pārdaugava by 1819, and in Zasulauks some of the houses built in the 1820s are still standing. Internal plumbing usually does not affect the facade. If the work changes the house from the outside, for example a new pipe along a wall or a ventilation stack outlet through the roof, find out beforehand which requirements apply to the particular house.

Apkaimes.lv: history of Āgenskalns and Ķīpsala, history of Torņakalns and Zasulauks. Checked 16.11.2026.

Ventilation without a new hole in the roof

Under LBN 221-15, a soil stack must extend at least 0.5 m above a pitched roof to ventilate the system. In an old wooden house where the toilet was moved or added later, a new vented stack means a hole in the roof. The building code allows, in buildings of up to five floors, a drain air admittance valve on the end of an unvented stack. This is provided the house keeps at least one stack with an outlet above the roof and the valve is above the rim of the highest connected fixture.

The valve must be accessible for maintenance and removable, so do not put it behind tight cladding. A ventilation stack must not be connected to room ventilation or a flue, which needs particular attention in old houses with stoves.

Cabinet Regulations No. 332, LBN 221-15, clauses 249, 251 and 280 (likumi.lv); UK Approved Document H, clause 1.33. Checked 16.11.2026.

A pipe by the outer wall: where a wooden house freezes

When water freezes its density falls by about 9%, so a pipe full of water can burst. In a wooden house pipes tend to run along an outer wall, under the floor or through an unheated hallway, and these places get cold first. In the advice from British water companies, pipes in unheated spaces are insulated without gaps, including at bends, valves and joints.

If a pipe has frozen anyway, first turn off the shut-off valve on the incoming supply pipe and leave open a tap through which the melted water can run out. Warm it slowly from the tap towards the frozen spot, with a heated towel or bottles of hot water. A naked flame and a blowtorch are forbidden in all this advice, and on a wooden wall they are also a fire risk. If the house will stand empty for a long time, close the shut-off valve on the incoming supply pipe and drain the pipes and the water heater.

USGS (water density); Affinity Water and WaterSafe (UK) advice on frozen pipes; Dražice water heater instructions on draining when there is a risk of frost. Checked 16.11.2026.

The incoming pipe under the yard: where Rīgas ūdens ends

Clause 3 of Riga City Council binding regulations No. 17 states that the Rīgas ūdens network includes the street water main and the service pipe up to the outer wall of the meter chamber (1.5–2 m from the street's red line) or to the plot boundary, and for drains up to the outer wall of the inspection chamber. Beyond that the pipes are the owner's responsibility, including the part that runs under the yard to the house.

In old wooden houses the incoming supply pipe may be galvanised steel. The US water utility EBMUD writes that as the zinc wears away the steel corrodes, rust narrows the pipe, and low pressure and brownish water result. An underground leak in a yard can be given away by wet patches, sunken paving or unusually green grass in dry weather, but not every leak is visible.

If the incoming pipe is replaced, under LBN 222-15 the external water pipe is laid at least 0.5 m deeper than the soil frost depth. Work on the city system is led by a qualified water supply and sewerage construction supervisor (regulations No. 17, clause 75). For a house with several flats, the whole property is connected to the city network, not an individual flat.

Riga City Council regulations No. 17, clauses 3, 7 and 75; LBN 222-15, clause 164 (likumi.lv); EBMUD; Yorkshire Water (signs of underground leaks). Checked 16.11.2026.

The cellar and shallow groundwater

At the northern end of Ķīpsala the groundwater is shallower than 1.5 m, as it is in the eastern part of Āgenskalns near Uzvaras parks and Klīversala and around Māras dīķis. If the cellar of a wooden house has a sink, shower or drain located lower than the cover of the nearest sewer inspection chamber, Riga's regulations (No. 17, clause 63.8) forbid connecting it without a non-return valve or an electrically operated gate valve.

Groundwater or rainwater may not be discharged into the drains without approval (clause 63.9), so do not connect a cellar drainage pump to the domestic drains on your own.

A water heater on a wooden partition

A full water heater weighs as many kilograms as it holds litres of water, plus the tank itself. In an old wooden house a partition can be boards with plaster or plasterboard added later, and such a wall may not carry this weight. The fixing needs a load-bearing wall, a post or a frame stud, or a smaller water heater or a floor-standing model has to be chosen.

Every pressurised water heater needs a safety valve on the cold water inlet, and it usually drips while the water heats. The manufacturer Ariston states that the drain pipe must be open to the air, pointing down and in a place where it does not freeze. In a wooden house the drain is led to the drains through an open funnel, not left to drip onto the floorboards.

Dražice OKCE/OKHE instructions (safety valve, dripping while heating); Ariston Velis Evo instructions (drain). Checked 16.11.2026.

What not to do in a wooden house

  • Do not thaw pipes with a naked flame or a blowtorch.
  • Do not connect a yard well or borehole pipe to the city water supply: regulations No. 17, clause 63.6 forbids it.
  • Do not put a new pipe in an outer wall or under the floor without insulation.
  • Do not hang a water heater on a thin partition without a strong fixing.
  • Do not change anything on the facade or roof in a protected area before the requirements are clear.

Riga City Council regulations No. 17, clauses 63.6 and 63.9 (likumi.lv). Checked 16.11.2026.

What people usually ask

The water pipe under the yard is leaking. Is it Rīgas ūdens's or mine?

Under clause 3 of regulations No. 17, the Rīgas ūdens network ends at the outer wall of the meter chamber or at the plot boundary. Beyond that, up to the house, the pipe is usually the owner's responsibility. If the leak is before the chamber, report it to Rīgas ūdens.

In an emergency, may I remove the meter seal to turn off the water?

Clause 64 of regulations No. 17 allows this in an emergency, but Rīgas ūdens must be notified within three working days at the latest.

Can a water heater be hung on the wall in a small wooden house?

It can, if the wall or frame stud can carry a full water heater. If the partition is thin, choose another wall, a smaller capacity or a floor-standing model. The safety valve drain is led to the drains through an open funnel.

There is an old well in the yard. Can it be connected to the house's water supply?

Connecting a borehole or well pipe to a house system that is connected to the city water supply is forbidden (regulations No. 17, clause 63.6). The well can be used separately, for example to water the garden with its own pump and its own pipes.
