Replacing radiators in a flat - is approval needed

Replacing a radiator in a flat in Āgenskalns or elsewhere in Pārdaugava, Riga's left bank, needs the building manager's approval if the building is heated from the shared heating. A radiator that runs on the building's heating is by law part of the common property, and without a calculation you may not significantly increase its output or change the connection scheme. A private house with its own boiler does not need such approval.

Why the radiator under your window is also the building's business

Section 4 of the Apartment Ownership Law counts the building's utility systems as common property, including heating elements inside a flat if their operation depends on the common-property utilities. In a building with central heating this usually applies to every radiator.

The reason is simple. Heating water runs up the riser through several floors, and every radiator on it affects how much heat is left for the neighbours above and below. A larger radiator or a different connection scheme can take heat from other flats or change the flow along the whole line.

Apartment Ownership Law, Sections 4 and 11 (likumi.lv); LV portāls e-consultation on approval of radiator replacement. Checked 16.11.2026.

Approval from the building manager before you buy the new radiator

  1. Write to the building manager State the flat, the room, the old radiator (cast-iron sections, steel panel) and the size and output of the new one from the manufacturer's data.
  2. Ask about output and scheme If the new radiator is significantly more powerful or the connection changes, the building manager may ask for a calculation. It is better to find this out before the purchase.
  3. Agree on shutting off the riser A radiator can be removed only when the heating riser has been shut off and drained. This is done by the building manager or a person they authorise, and in the heating season also to their schedule.
  4. Fitting and refilling After fitting, the riser is refilled, the radiator is bled and the connections are checked. The riser is opened by the same person who shut it off.
  5. Keep the approval A written answer will be useful if a neighbour later complains about a cool flat or if you sell the flat.

What the new radiator must keep the same as the old one

The simplest to approve is a like-for-like replacement: similar output, the same place, the same supply pipes. In many older buildings the radiator is connected to a riser through which water continues on to the next floor. In such a scheme there is a bypass pipe under the radiator that lets the water pass if the radiator valve is closed.

If the bypass is removed or a thermostatic head is fitted without it, a closed radiator can block the flow for the neighbours too. That is why the building manager usually wants to know not only the radiator model but also how it will be connected. If you want to move the radiator to a different wall, the scheme changes, and it is no longer a simple replacement.

Underfloor heating from central heating

An underfloor heating loop from the building's heating riser changes the connection scheme, and the LV portāls explanation says this may not be done without a calculation. It also affects the common property, so under Section 11 of the Apartment Ownership Law the flat owners' association must consent. Ask the building manager before the project, not after the tiling.

Technically, a floor needs a lower water temperature than a radiator. According to Uponor materials based on EN 1264, a floor in a living room must not warm above 29 °C, in a bathroom usually not above 33 °C. A floor connected directly to a hot heating riser easily exceeds these limits. Electric underfloor heating is not connected to the building's heating, but it is installed by an electrician.

EN 1264 floor surface temperature limits according to the Uponor technical handbook and Uponor Siccus 14 technical information; Apartment Ownership Law, Section 11. Checked 16.11.2026.

What not to do until the building manager answers

  • Do not shut off or drain the heating riser in the basement yourself.
  • Do not remove the old radiator in the heating season while the system is full and the riser is not shut off.
  • Do not buy a significantly more powerful radiator until it is clear whether a calculation is needed.
  • Do not remove the bypass pipe or fit a thermostatic head without it on a riser that continues to the neighbours.
  • Do not connect an underfloor heating pipe to the heating riser without approval.

The labour price for one radiator and what changes it

Fitting or replacing a radiator is from €75. The price includes removing the old radiator, hanging the new one on the same supply pipes, refilling the system and bleeding the air. Shutting off the riser is arranged by the building manager and is not in this price. This is the labour price; materials are extra.

The price changes with the weight and material of the old radiator (heavy cast iron has to be carried down the stairs). It also changes with whether the valves and bypass have to be replaced, and whether the supply pipes are old steel pipe that has to be adapted. If there is a travel charge, it is stated on the call before the work. More on the work: radiator replacement, all prices: prices.

What people usually ask

Is approval needed even if I only change the radiator valve or thermostatic head?

If the job requires the heating riser to be shut off and drained, it cannot be done without the building manager. A thermostatic head on an existing thermostatic valve can be fitted without draining water, but on a riser without a bypass it can affect the neighbours, so it is better to ask.

May I move a radiator to a different wall?

That is a change of the connection scheme, not a like-for-like replacement. The LV portāls explanation says the scheme may not be changed without a calculation, and the consent of the owners' association is usually needed too, because the work affects the common property.

The building is replacing all the radiators. Do I have to pay for mine?

That depends on the association's decision and the management agreement. Unless the building's documents say otherwise, such work is usually financed as work for the whole building. Ask the building manager in writing.

Does a private house in Bieriņi with its own boiler need any approval too?

The Apartment Ownership Law does not apply to a separate private house, so no approval from a building manager is needed. It is worth comparing the output with what the boiler can supply, and after replacement the system has to be refilled to the pressure stated by the boiler manufacturer.
